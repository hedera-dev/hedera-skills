# Discovery Question Bank

Ask in small batches (2-3 questions), not all at once — this is a
conversation, not a form. Always ask for a number when the client mentions
pain ("a few days" → "how many days exactly, last time it happened?").

## Gate question (ask first, always)

> Are there 2 or more parties in this process who today do NOT fully trust
> each other, and who need to see the same data without depending on one of
> them to confirm it?

If the answer is a single controlling party with no external counterparty who
needs independent proof, stop here and route to the disqualification branch
in SKILL.md Phase 1 — do not continue to the batches below.

## Batch 1 — The process today

1. Walk me through the process step by step, exactly as it happens today.
2. Who executes each step, and roughly how much of their time does it take?
3. What systems does this already run on? (ERP, spreadsheets, paper, email,
   shared folders)

## Batch 2 — The pain, quantified

4. When was the last time this process caused a real problem — a dispute, a
   failed audit, a delayed payment, a fraud scare? What happened, concretely?
5. How long did resolving that take, in hours or days-of-work? Get a number.
6. If this happens again next month, what would it cost you (money, time,
   reputation with a specific client)?

## Batch 3 — Scale and counterparties

7. What volume are we talking about? (units/transactions per month)
8. Who's on the other side of the trust gap? (a specific client, a regulator,
   a supplier, a partner) Would they actually value being able to verify this
   themselves, or do they not care?
9. Is there one especially demanding counterparty (strictest client, toughest
   auditor) worth piloting with first?

## Batch 4 — Readiness to pilot

10. If we scoped a small pilot — one client, a few weeks, a capped budget —
    who internally would need to say yes?
11. What's a budget ceiling you'd be comfortable with to test this before
    scaling? (helps size the MVP realistically)
12. What would have to be true at the end of a pilot for you to say "yes,
    scale this"? (get this before building anything — it becomes the
    go/no-go criteria in Phase 3)

## Tips

- If the client can't quantify the pain, that's a signal — either dig one
  level deeper on a recent concrete incident, or treat it as a weaker
  candidate pattern and look at another row in the catalog.
- Non-technical clients often answer in terms of *feelings* ("it's a hassle",
  "we don't fully trust it") — translate those into the closest quantifiable
  proxy (hours, disputes/year, deals lost) before writing the MVP template.
- Never let the client leave the call without an answer to question 12 — a
  pilot with no agreed success criteria cannot be evaluated later.
